Duolingo for AI: what the phrase really means

People who search for “Duolingo for AI” usually want an app with short daily sessions, a path, and quizzes, instead of a long course. Sapaude is built that way: a guided path, quizzes, and one briefing, from about five minutes a day, in German and English. Sapaude is not Duolingo and is not connected to it. Duolingo is a trademark of Duolingo, Inc.

The phrase names a shape, not a test result. There is no single app for everyone. The other shapes are on which app fits for learning AI.

What the comparison gets right

A language app people open every day has a recognisable shape: the next step is already there, the session is short, and a quiz checks whether the sentence stuck. Sapaude uses that shape for AI fundamentals.

  • A path in three levels: Basics, Core, Depth. You start where you stand.
  • Short lessons and quizzes. New words are explained in plain language the first time they appear.
  • A length you set: 5, 10, or 15 minutes. One reminder stays on the device. There is no clock to pick.
  • German and English. You switch the language in the app.

The activity screen shows daily missions and a streak. The streak starts at zero. How the streak is counted is in the app, not a promise on this page.

What the comparison gets wrong

Sapaude does not teach a foreign language, and it is not a stack of prompt flashcards. The subject is AI: what a model does, what a token is, and how you tell what a news item does not prove.

It also adds something a language app does not: a daily briefing in our own words, with a source, matched to your level in the app. The long texts are on this site too, under Briefing. Listening to the briefing is part of Sapaude Pro. Listening in Basics 1 and 2 is free.

Sapaude is not “the” app, and not the winner of a comparison test. Other products use the same phrase for prompt drills or for programming courses. Those are different jobs. A programming app, for example Sololearn, practises code. A problem-solving app, for example Brilliant, practises thinking through problems. Sapaude practises understanding and reading the news.

The store listings are not live yet. Until they are public, the home page says “coming soon”, with no link.

One day, in practice

You open the app for the session you set. The lesson explains one idea in plain language, then a quiz. Beside it is the briefing: curated AI news in our own words. On the website you can read the same long texts without the app. Three examples: Gemini glasses on preorder, Notion AI chats got exposed, and Chatbots cite fake shops.

Basics 1 and 2, including listening, are free. Sapaude Pro is monthly or yearly. You see the prices in the app before you buy. Only the yearly plan has a one-week trial. The monthly plan has none. Apple sells and bills it. The app does not require a name. Progress stays on the device.

If you want to write code, or you want a long course, this shape is too small. Then Sapaude is not the honest answer, even when the comparison sounds neat.

Common questions

Is Sapaude a Duolingo for AI?

Only in the shape: a path, short sessions, quizzes, from about five minutes a day. Sapaude is not a language course, not a Duolingo product, and not connected to Duolingo. Duolingo is a trademark of Duolingo, Inc.

What do you practise in a day?

You choose 5, 10, or 15 minutes. There is also a briefing, matched to your level in the app. Listening in Basics 1 and 2 is free. Listening to the briefing is part of Sapaude Pro.

Do you need to code?

No. Sapaude explains fundamentals in plain language. If you want to write code, you need a programming app. That is a different shape.
