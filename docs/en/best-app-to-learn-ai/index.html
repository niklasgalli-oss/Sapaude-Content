What is the best app to learn AI?

There is no single app for everyone. “Learn AI” means different jobs: fundamentals in plain language, writing code, practising prompts, or reading the day’s news. Sapaude is a learning app for AI fundamentals and one briefing a day, in German and English, from about five minutes. Basics 1 and 2 are free, including listening.

This page is not a test and not a ranking. It says which shape fits which job, and which of those jobs Sapaude covers. Other apps’ prices, stars, and user counts are not here, because we did not measure them.

Name the job before you name the app

Understand the basics. You want to say what a model does, what a token is, and where a news claim fails. That needs short explanations and repetition, not a catalogue of tools.

Talk about it at work. A meeting uses a word you have not met. You want to place it next time without a semester of catch-up. A short path plus a briefing matched to what you already know is the fit.

Write code. You want scripts or a project of your own. A programming app is the right shape. Sapaude does not replace it.

Practise prompts. You already know what you want from a chat, and you want to train the wording. That is a different drill from a fundamentals path.

When Sapaude fits

Sapaude fits if you want to learn the subject the way many people learn a language: a path, short sessions, quizzes, and the day’s news in our own words. You choose from 5 min a day: 5, 10, or 15. One reminder stays on the device. The app sets its time from when you usually open Sapaude. There is no clock to pick.

The path has three levels. Basics explains what AI is, in plain language. Core gives the parts their names, each explained the first time. Depth is longer articles: how a story is built, and how you tell what it does not prove.

The briefing is curated and written in our own words. In the app it matches your level. The same long texts are fixed HTML on this site, under Briefing. Listening to the briefing is part of Sapaude Pro. Listening in Basics 1 and 2 is free.

The app does not require a name. Reading progress, quiz progress, bookmarks, settings, and an optional name stay on the device. This website sets no cookies and loads no third-party trackers.

Sapaude for Teams is a waitlist, not a finished product. The EU AI Act (Article 4) asks companies to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among their staff. That is the frame, not a certificate Sapaude issues. The waitlist is support@sapaude.app.

When another shape is the honest one

If you want code in an editor by the end of the week, use a programming app. A briefing does not teach syntax.

If you want puzzles in maths or science, a problem-solving app is closer. Sapaude practises how to read AI, not calculus.

If you only want a stack of prompt templates, a drill format is shorter. Sapaude explains first what a model can do and what a source actually shows.

If you need a university course, a paper, or a certification, an app is the wrong place. Sapaude is a learning app for ordinary days, not a university and not an exam.

How to tell in a week

You do not need a store listing or a comparison test. Three questions work for any app you open.

  1. After a lesson, can you explain one term in a sentence without only repeating the word? The home page shows the move: “context window” and “tokens” become “A model can only hold a certain amount of text in view at once.”
  2. Given a news item, can you say what the source shows and what it does not? The long briefing texts are built for that: one point on what the source shows, one on the limit of the claim, one on what to check next.
  3. Can you work in five to fifteen minutes, in German or English? If you want an hour and a project, the frame is too small.

Two texts that show the difference: Gemini glasses on preorder explains a device and the limit on accounts and privacy. Chatbots cite fake shops says where the number comes from and what the test does not prove.
